Windward Point is a small fishing town on the Amberlee Coast. We want a one-page site
that reads like a short guide to the place, told in chapters, for someone deciding whether to
visit.

Opening chapter: Windward Point sits where a narrow tidal inlet meets the open water, its harbor
lined with weathered clapboard houses painted in faded blues and ochres. The town was built
around cod fishing in the 1800s and still keeps a small working fleet, though most visitors now
come for the quiet: the slap of rigging against masts, gulls circling the fish market at dawn,
and a walkable center small enough to see on foot in an afternoon.

Chapter: The Harbor. The working harbor is the heart of the town. Boats unload their catch each
morning around 6am at the public dock, and the fish market beside it sells the day's catch
straight off the boats until it sells out, usually by mid-morning. A short breakwater walk gives
a view back over the whole harbor and the lighthouse beyond it.

Chapter: The Old Lighthouse District. Above the harbor, a cluster of nineteenth-century keeper's
cottages surrounds the Windward Point Light, a still-active lighthouse first lit in 1868. The
cottages are private homes now, but the lane between them is open to walkers, and the light itself
is open for a short climb on weekend afternoons.

Chapter: Tide Pool Trail. A mile-long footpath runs along the rocky shore south of the harbor,
past tide pools that fill with starfish, anemones and hermit crabs at low tide. The trail is flat
and easy, with benches every quarter mile, and it ends at a small headland with a view out over
the open water.

Chapter: The Market Quarter. A few streets back from the harbor, the Market Quarter holds the
town's weekly outdoor market, held every Saturday morning, selling local produce, preserves and
crafts from the surrounding coast.

Places to see: the Windward Point Light (the still-active 1868 lighthouse), the public fish
market beside the harbor dock, the Tide Pool Trail's headland viewpoint, and the Saturday market
in the Market Quarter.

Practical facts: getting there — the nearest airport is Amberlee Regional, about 40 minutes away
by car, and there is no train station in town. Best time to visit — late spring through early
autumn, when the tide pools are most active and the market runs every week; winter storms close
the breakwater walk. Currency — the town uses the same currency as the rest of the coast, with
cards accepted almost everywhere. Language — English is spoken throughout the town.
