Riverkeepers Trust is a local charity that cleans, restores and watches over the rivers of the Wye
valley with volunteers. We want a one-page site that gets visitors to donate or volunteer.

Nav: Our work, Get involved, Events, About us, with a "Donate" call to action.

Hero: "Our rivers are choking. Volunteers are bringing them back." Farm run-off and plastic have
turned stretches of the Wye and its streams green and still. Every weekend, local people pull out
rubbish, plant banks and test the water. Buttons: "Donate today" and "Join a clean-up".

Why we exist: we believe a river belongs to everyone who lives along it, and that the people who
live along it are best placed to look after it. We train volunteers, restore riverbanks and
publish what the water tests show, so the whole valley can see what is changing.

What we do, three programmes run by volunteers with a small staff team behind them: clean-ups
(monthly work days that clear rubbish and invasive weeds from the banks), bank planting (native
trees and reeds that hold the soil and shade the water), citizen testing (trained volunteers
sample the water each week and publish the results).

Last year on the river, from our annual report, counted by volunteers and checked by staff: 640
volunteers, 18 tonnes of rubbish cleared, 4,200 trees planted, 1,150 water samples.

Stories from the bank: "I came for one Saturday clean-up. Three years later I run the testing team
for our stretch." — Delyth Morgan, Volunteer, Hay-on-Wye. "The kingfishers came back to the bend
below our farm the second summer after the planting." — Gareth Pryce, Farmer, Builth Wells.

Where your money goes: registered charity number 1180000 in England and Wales; 82 pence in every
pound goes straight to river work; accounts independently examined every year.
