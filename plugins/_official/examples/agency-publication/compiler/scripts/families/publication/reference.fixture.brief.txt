Field Notes Weekly is an independent weekly magazine and newsletter about small farms, food and
the people who grow it. We want a one-page site that gets a reader to subscribe or become a
member.

farm in the Welsh borders stopped chasing supermarket varieties. Ten years on, its heritage cider
apples sell out before the blossom falls. By Morwenna Hale.

Latest stories: "Why the Saturday market moved to Thursday evenings" (Markets, 12 September) —
traders swapped the weekend crowd for commuters, and takings went up. "A no-dig bed, one season
later" (Growing, 5 September) — what worked, what rotted, and what a first-year grower would
change. "Cooking the glut: forty ways with courgettes" (Kitchens, 29 August) — readers sent their
best late-summer recipes; we tested the top ten. "Hedgerows are back on the balance sheet" (Land,
22 August) — how new grants are changing the sums for farms that plant field edges.

Running series: First Harvest (new growers on their first full season), The Long Table (one
recipe from one farm, every fortnight), Ground Truth (soil, water and the numbers behind them),
Market Day (portraits of the stalls that keep towns fed).

From the archive: "The seed library under the village hall" (Spring issue) — how a shoebox of
saved beans became a lending library for three counties. "Lambing in the dark months" (Winter
issue) — a week on a hill farm where the busiest nights fall in January. "The last miller on the
river" (Autumn issue) — a water mill that still grinds flour for the bakers down the valley.
"Bees, borders and the neighbours" (Summer issue) — a beekeeper and an arable farmer on sharing
the same fields.

Who writes Field Notes: Morwenna Hale, Editor for farming and markets — grew up on a dairy farm
and has covered rural economics for fifteen years. Tobias Achebe, Food and kitchens — a former
restaurant cook who now writes about what small kitchens do with a harvest. Ilse Varga, Land and
climate — a soil scientist turned reporter, happiest with a spade and a spreadsheet.
