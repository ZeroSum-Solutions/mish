The Kestrel Point Museum of Maritime Craft is a museum of boatbuilding, sailmaking and harbour life at 22 Commercial Wharf in Portland, Maine. Visitors are deciding whether a visit is worth their afternoon, so lead with what is on now: three exhibitions, "Lines and Lofting" (through November 15), "Salt and Canvas" (through November 29) and "The Working Harbor" (through January 10). The permanent collection fills five galleries: Small Craft (86 boats), Tools of the Trade (1,240 objects), Sails and Rigging (310 objects), Charts and Instruments (520 objects) and Harbor Photographs (4,100 prints). Upcoming events: a late opening on Thursday, October 8, 6 to 8 p.m.; a family knot-tying day on Saturday, October 17, 10 a.m. to 2 p.m.; a curator talk on Wednesday, October 28, 6:30 to 7:30 p.m.; and a closing walk through "Lines and Lofting" on Sunday, November 15, 2 to 3 p.m. Programmes include a Saturday workshop for ages 8 to 12 and an adult evening boatbuilding course. The museum is open Wednesday to Sunday, 10 a.m. to 5 p.m. Admission is eighteen dollars for adults, fifteen for seniors, and free under 18; the Metro bus route 8 stops at the wharf. Membership is sixty-five dollars a year for an individual, one hundred ten for a household, or three hundred as a patron. Contact visit@kestrelpointmuseum.org or (207) 555-0142. The primary call to action is planning a visit.
